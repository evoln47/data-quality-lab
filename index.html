Survey Data Quality Lab

A brand tracker fields 2,400 completes, and the share of those completes that survives quality control decides what the headline number means. This Lab prices that decision. Toggle the checks a research team runs, watch the headline move toward a true value the simulation knows by construction, and see what each provider's completes cost once the unusable ones come out. Every dataset on this page is simulated.

Assumptions

  • All respondent data on this page is simulated. A seeded generator (mulberry32, seed 20261123) builds 2,400 completes per provider from five respondent strata, so every visitor sees the same numbers.
  • The survey is a brand-health tracker for Northwind Coffee, a fictional coffee chain. The headline measure is brand consideration: the share of respondents who would consider Northwind for their next coffee purchase.
  • The true headline value is the brand consideration rate among attentive respondents in the simulated data. It is known because the data is generated. Field data has no equivalent.
  • Provider prices default to $6.50, $4.00, and $1.80 per complete for Providers A, B, and C. The providers are fictional, and their respondent mixes are illustrative.
  • The speeder screen defaults to 3.0 minutes. The attentive median completion time in the simulated data is 9.0 minutes, so the default threshold sits at about a third of it.
  • Two percentages in §03, 55.0% and 75.3%, are program figures from Evan Volness's open-end coding work. The 40 responses in that section are written for this page. The section keeps the program figures and the example apart.

04

What cleaning cannot fix

Cleaning acts on completes that already exist. Every check in §01 judges a finished interview. A person who never opened the invitation, or who closed the survey at the second question, leaves nothing for a check to judge. Nonresponse of that kind moves a tracker when the people who answer differ from the people who stay silent, and no toggle on this page touches it.

Coverage sits upstream of every check. A panel reaches the people who joined it. Shoppers outside the panel, including the customers a brand most wants to win, never enter the 2,400 completes at all. Weighting can rebalance a sample toward known population margins, and it cannot create answers from people the frame never held.

The Lab models data quality after collection. Representation, the question of whose answers the completes stand for, sits outside the model, and a cleaned headline can be precise about the wrong population. The gap to truth in §01 measures distance from the attentive respondents inside the simulation. It says nothing about distance from the market outside it.

05

Notes on the model

Simplification Strata are pure types. Each simulated respondent belongs to exactly one stratum, so every failure has one cause. Field respondents mix failure modes: a fast respondent can still answer with care, and an attentive respondent can paste one open-end. The ladder's clean separation is a property of the simulation, stated here so the separation is read as a teaching device.

Arithmetic Cost per usable complete divides the full field cost (price per complete times 2,400) by the completes that survive. It leaves out the analyst time cleaning takes. Open-end review therefore reads as free in the cost column while taking the most analyst time of any check on the page.

Arithmetic The true value is a mean over one stratum. It is computed from the attentive respondents in the selected provider's simulated data, so it moves by small sampling amounts when the provider changes. The population value behind the attentive stratum is 38.0%.

Simplification Checks judge flags, and flags judge proxies. The speeder screen sees a time, and time stands in for attention. The straightline screen sees a pattern, and the pattern stands in for disengagement. Each proxy misclassifies in both directions, which is why the cleaned headline approaches the true value and stops short of it.

Provider mixes are illustrative. The published platform study in Methodology §05 reports pass rates from real fieldwork. The Lab's three providers borrow the shape of that spread and nothing else; no provider on this page describes a real company.

The §03 example is sized for reading. Forty responses make each routing change visible as a handful of flipped rows. Production coding runs over thousands of responses, where the same changes move the agreement rate by fractions of a point at a time and the audit sample does the persuading.

Dataset generation

A seeded mulberry32 generator (seed 20261123, with one stream per provider) draws 2,400 respondents per provider. Each respondent first draws a stratum from the provider's mix, then draws answers from that stratum's behavior settings. The seed fixes every draw, so the page produces identical figures for every visitor and every figure can be recomputed from this description.

02

Cleaning mechanics

Each respondent holds one flag per check. The attention flag records a missed instructed-response item. The speeder flag compares completion time with the threshold currently set. The straightline flag records an identical run down a full grid battery. The demographic flag records an age answer and a life-stage answer that disagree. The open-end flag records text the generator marked as fabricated or pasted.

A check removes every respondent whose flag it owns. Checks combine as a union: a respondent flagged by any active check leaves the data once. Survival, headline, and composition figures are computed over the respondents who remain, and the true value is computed over the attentive stratum before any check runs.

03

Cost arithmetic

Field cost equals price per complete times 2,400 raw completes. Cost per usable complete equals field cost divided by surviving completes, which is the same as price per complete divided by the survival rate. Editing the price in §01 reprices the field at the same survival rate, so the cost column moves in direct proportion to the price.

In §02 the accuracy bar adds one rule: a provider meets the bar when the absolute gap between its cleaned headline and the true value is at or under the bar. The shaded cost cell marks the lowest cost per usable complete among providers meeting the bar. A provider can therefore post the lowest cost in the table and still miss the shading, which is the comparison the section exists to show.

04

The coding example

The 40 responses in §03 were written for this page. Each holds a human code, a first-pass machine code, and the step at which the machine code reaches the human code, if it ever does. Agreement at a step counts the responses whose machine code at that step equals the human code. The steps mirror the routing changes in the program work: a clarified codeframe settles category boundary cases, routing low-confidence cases to a human settles ambiguous ones, and sampling disagreements for audit finds recurring patterns the first two steps miss.

The program figures stand apart from the example. In production, agreement moved from 55.0% to 75.3% across the same three changes, measured over the program's full coded volume. The example is built to land near those endpoints so the mechanics stay legible at 40 responses.

05

Limits and evidence sources

Limits. The simulation knows its truth because it generates its respondents. That device makes the gap measurable, and it also bounds every claim on the page: the Lab shows how cleaning moves a number whose truth is knowable, while fieldwork cleaning moves a number whose truth stays estimated. Strata are pure types, thresholds are single numbers, and the cost model prices completes and nothing else.

Evidence sources

  • Matheny, L. M., et al. (2026). Data Quality in Online Crowdsourced Surveys: Methodological Challenges and Analytic Insights in Survey Research. Survey Practice, May 20, 2026. One survey fielded on three paid platforms, scored with 20 quality checks; the share of completes passing every check ran 70.5%, 61.8%, and 10.2% by platform. The Lab's fictional providers borrow the shape of that spread.
  • Instructed-response attention items, completion-time speeder screens, and straightline detection on grid batteries are standard instruments in the survey methodology literature on online data quality; the checks in §01 implement them in their common forms.
  • Program figures in §03 (55.0% and 75.3% machine and human coding agreement) are from Evan Volness's open-end coding work, stated here as program results. The 40 example responses are illustrative.
